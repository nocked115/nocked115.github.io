Unsupervised Medical Image Anomaly Detection

Mar – Sep 2026 · Undergraduate researcher, Dongduk Women's University · Co-first author (one of three, six-author team) · Led the liver CT track · Rejected at IEEE Sensors Journal; preparing resubmission

My contribution

  • Owned the liver CT domain end to end: preprocessing (BTCV + LiTS), convolutional autoencoder, and PaDiM with position-wise Gaussian modeling.
  • Implemented validation-guided score fusion and compared adaptive, fixed and quantile thresholds.
  • Compared ResNet-18/34/50 and WideResNet-50 backbones.
  • Selected checkpoints by validation image AUROC, never by test results.
  • Merged the three domains into one manuscript and handled Q&A with the advising professor.

What I found

Test-set leakage in score normalization. Scores were min–max normalized with the test set's own statistics. I found it, proposed fixing all three domains to validation-set statistics, and we reran every experiment. AUROC moved by at most 0.0011, so the conclusions held, but now they were earned. A good score does not prove a fair evaluation.
Lesions lost by downsampling. Pixel AUROC on liver CT came out at 0.02. Tracing the code showed small lesions vanishing when masks were shrunk to 64×64, so the ground truth no longer matched the image. Restoring the mask handling brought it back above 0.90. What survives preprocessing decides what can be evaluated at all.
Fusion did not beat reconstruction-only on liver CT. The tuned weight leaned almost entirely toward reconstruction (α* = 0.90), so ImageNet features added little there. We reported the drop rather than hiding it, and listed resolution loss, domain mismatch and LiTS slices that share 3D volumes across validation and test as limitations.

What I found

Timing is part of correctness. A detector that fires after the scene is shown has failed, however accurate it is. So I measured latency against human-marked onsets: p90 0.133 s, well inside a 3-second buffer that ran at 720p without dropped frames.
Speed was solved; evidence was not. The audio detector caught 35% of jump scares, and 93% of its alarms were false. On 24 ordinary videos it fired 122 times an hour, the same rate as on horror clips, so its alarms were not tracking scares. Rejecting speech segments cut test-set false positives by 63%. A fast detector can still be listening to the wrong thing.

What Must Survive: Skeleton-Preserving Visual Token Reduction In preparation

Nov 2026 – · Study club team project · I proposed the topic, which was selected as the club's project
  • Question: if a VLM keeps only the visual tokens around a person's skeleton, can it still understand what the person is doing?
  • Interesting failure cases: drinking water and answering a phone have almost the same skeleton motion; the object in the hand decides. Running means something different on a track and on a road.
  • Goal: find which actions the skeleton alone explains, and which object or scene tokens must also survive.

Other work

  • VLM paper study. Presented GLIP and Grounding DINO, comparing how they ground language in images; studied CLIP-style image–text contrastive learning.
  • Departure alert from driving video (OpenCV, SKT FLY AI, one-day team project). Motion and traffic-light cues; tested on day and night video.
  • Clickbait news detection (poster, Korea Multimedia Society 2026 · paper). Validation F1 was 0.98, but on a test set I built myself it fell to 0.46: the model had memorized patterns, not checked headline–article consistency. Consistency features brought the harder split to F1 0.7617.

Research direction

  • Reliable visual perception: models that keep deciding correctly when the input changes (resolution, domain, lighting), and evaluation that checks whether they decided for the right reasons.
  • What must survive: which visual information a model truly needs, so vision-language models can be made efficient without losing the evidence behind their decisions.
  • Long term: perception for autonomous driving and robots, where a model that is right for the wrong reason can be dangerous.
